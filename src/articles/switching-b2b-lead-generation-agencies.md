You have paid for lead generation, but the campaign is leaving your sales team with weak appointments, incomplete records or too much follow-up to manage. A new provider may help. Before moving, establish what needs to change and what your business needs to retain.

This guide is for B2B companies replacing a lead generation agency, cold calling provider or outsourced SDR team on a campaign serving Canada or the United States. It explains the decisions to settle with the outgoing provider, your salespeople and the incoming team.

## Establish why the current campaign needs to change

Begin with a sample of recent accounts, conversations and meetings. Compare what the provider agreed to deliver with what reached your sellers, including the context behind appointments that were accepted or rejected.

Weak performance can arise from several places: the wrong market, an unclear offer, poor calling, shallow qualification, missing follow-up or slow action inside your business. Record the evidence behind the problem. A provider change will need to address those conditions in the next campaign brief.

| Observed problem | What to inspect before choosing a replacement |
| --- | --- |
| Appointments with unsuitable companies | Target-account criteria and exclusions |
| Buyers who cannot explain the meeting | Call message, agreement to meet and confirmation |
| Relevant prospects disappearing after contact | Follow-up ownership and promised next actions |
| Sellers repeating the first conversation | Qualification notes and handoff requirements |
| Lots of activity with little useful learning | Reporting detail and the campaign review process |

Ask your salespeople for specific examples. Their experience is particularly useful when the reports look healthy but the appointments do not progress.

## Review the agreement before setting a handover date

Check the current agreement for notice, remaining charges, termination arrangements and access to campaign materials. Have the responsible person in your business confirm how those terms apply before promising a start date to another provider.

Separate what your company already owns from licensed data, agency-owned tools and materials created under the engagement. Request written clarification where continued access or reuse is uncertain. This gives the incoming team a realistic view of what it can use.

A useful transition schedule identifies the final day of new outreach by the outgoing provider, responsibility for later replies and the point at which account ownership changes. Allow for appointments booked before the change but scheduled to take place afterwards.

## Request a handover that a new team can use

A contact export needs context. The incoming team should be able to tell which accounts were approached, what the buyer said and what action remains outstanding.

Use the checklist below to request the records your business is entitled to receive. Confirm the format and coverage of each export before relying on it.

| Record or material | Why the incoming team needs it |
| --- | --- |
| Company and contact records | Establish the account, role, territory and available contact details |
| Recent outreach history | Understand the latest conversation and avoid unnecessary repeat contact |
| Open replies and callbacks | Continue commitments that still require a response |
| Booked appointments | Preserve the agreed time, purpose, attendees and confirmation status |
| Contact exclusions | Prevent outreach to people and accounts marked for suppression |
| Approved messages and claims | Review the offer as it was represented, subject to reuse rights |
| Campaign definitions and reports | Understand how earlier results were counted |
| System and access inventory | Identify the accounts, integrations and permissions requiring review |

If a record cannot be transferred, document the gap and decide how the affected work will be handled. For CRM field mapping and record ownership, use the [appointment-setting CRM integration guide](/articles/appointment-setting-crm-integration/).

## Give every active buyer conversation an owner

Work through the open queue before starting fresh outreach. A prospect who has asked for a technical answer, requested a callback or accepted a meeting should have one clear route back to your business.

The following routing plan is an example your team can adapt. Assign named people and actual dates in the working version.

| Buyer status | Handover action |
| --- | --- |
| Meeting already booked | Confirm the seller, calendar entry and prospect expectations |
| Waiting for a product answer | Assign the question to a client specialist and retain the deadline |
| Requested a later callback | Transfer the reason and agreed timing to the responsible team |
| In an active sales opportunity | Keep ownership with the account executive unless explicitly reassigned |
| Requested no further contact | Preserve the exclusion in the systems that control outreach |
| Interest or ownership unclear | Review the record before putting it into a new sequence |

Where both agencies remain involved briefly, divide the work explicitly. The same account should not enter two uncoordinated calling queues. Tell your own sellers who handles late replies from the previous campaign.

## Check the imported records before reconnecting outreach

Review a representative sample after the transfer. Confirm that account owners, timestamps, meeting details and exclusions survived the export and import, and that the new team can interpret the notes.

Pay attention to duplicate contacts, conflicting account assignments and fields that lost their meaning between systems. A status such as “qualified” needs an explanation of what the earlier team actually confirmed. Retain the distinction between buyer statements and caller assumptions.

Your systems owner should review access and integrations as part of the handover. Grant the new team the permissions it needs and remove old access at the agreed point after checking dependencies. Where missing suppression information leaves contact eligibility uncertain, hold the affected records for review.

## Compare replacement providers against the problem you found

Give each prospective partner the same brief. Explain the offer, target market, previous scope, failure evidence and the responsibilities your business will keep.

Ask how the provider would handle the specific gap. For weak qualification, request a sample meeting record and acceptance process. For poor account coverage, ask how research and caller allocation would be planned. For inconsistent management, establish who reviews the campaign and how changes reach the callers.

Compare proposals against your transition needs as well as the ongoing service.

If the alternative is bringing the work inside your company, the [outsourced SDR versus in-house team guide](/articles/outsourced-sdr-vs-in-house-sdr-team/) covers that separate decision.

## Agree on a controlled restart

Before the new team calls, approve the account criteria, offer language, qualification requirements and route for buyer questions. Give callers access to appropriate training and examples of useful sales conversations.

Start with a reviewed group of accounts and inspect the early records. Check whether the team understood the audience, preserved exclusions, captured the buyer response and assigned the next action correctly. Use the findings to adjust the campaign before expanding its workload.

For Canadian and US accounts, confirm the territories, prospect time zones, language needs and seller availability. Agree on the applicable contact rules for the intended channels. Avoid carrying a previous provider's assumptions into the new scope without review.

A restart plan should also state what happens to records that remain unresolved. Keep them visible in a separate review queue with an owner and a decision date.

## Decide how you will judge the replacement

Agree on reporting definitions before comparing the old and new results. A booked appointment, an attended meeting and a sales-accepted opportunity describe different stages, and each needs a consistent meaning.

Look at a comparable audience and campaign period where possible. A change in account size, offer, territory or qualification threshold can alter results independently of the provider. Record those differences so the review remains useful.

Bring sales feedback into the discussion. Are meeting notes helping sellers prepare? Are the right people attending? Is the new team resolving the failure that triggered the change?
