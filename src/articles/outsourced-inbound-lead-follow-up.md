An operations director requests a demo. She needs a better way to approve purchases across three locations. Your reps are in meetings, so CallTeam makes the first call.

## What CallTeam says on the first call

> “Hi, Maya. You asked for a demo and mentioned purchase approvals across three locations. What do you need the software to handle?”

Maya says each site has a different approver. We ask where requests get stuck. If she needs an integration, we record which system she uses and what data needs to move. Your specialist can prepare an answer before speaking with her.

We bring the same phone skills we use in [B2B cold calling](/services/b2b-cold-calling/). We lead with the buyer’s own request and listen for what they need next.

## What we do when the buyer misses our call

If Maya is tied up, we can leave a live voicemail or play an approved voicemail drop, as agreed. We give her a reason to call back and log the attempt.

> “Hi, Maya. This is Jordan calling about the demo you requested. I wanted to check which approval steps you need us to show. You can call me back on this number.”

We call again at the agreed time or try another window within your follow-up plan. If Maya has left a callback request, the next caller can see it and pick up where we left off. If she declines or asks us to stop, we close the follow-up and log the outcome.

## When we put a meeting on your calendar

We use the criteria you agree with us. For a software demo, we check account fit, what the buyer wants to see and who should attend. For a quote, we may ask what work they need, where they need it and when the price is due.

If Maya’s IT lead needs to check the integration, we look for a time that works for both. If Maya needs an answer before she can schedule, we route the question to your specialist and record who will call her back.

A quote request may need a different next step. Say a buyer wants a site visit within two weeks. We confirm the location and the scope of the visit, then check who can take it. If the work falls outside your service area, we record the reason so your team can close the request.

Use the [software demo qualification guide](/articles/qualify-software-demo-before-booking/) to choose what your caller should ask. Try the questions against a real enquiry before you set the meeting criteria.

## What your seller receives

Before the demo, your seller gets the original request, what the caller learned and what the buyer agreed to. We flag anything the seller still needs to confirm.

**Sample sales note for this example**

- **Requested.** A demo of purchase approvals for three locations.
- **Show.** How each location gets its own approver and what happens when that person is away.
- **Who’s joining.** Maya from Operations and her IT lead at the agreed time.
- **Open question.** Which purchasing system they use and what an integration would need to pass back.

The seller can open the demo with the workflow Maya asked to see. In your [appointment-setting CRM workflow](/articles/appointment-setting-crm-integration/), agree where this note goes so the seller can find it before the meeting.

## What we agree before the calls start

Pick the enquiries you want us to work. A demo form, a request for pricing and a referral may each call for a different conversation. For a narrow first campaign, start with one source and give the caller the original message, contact details and any notes the buyer supplied.

Agree which hours we cover, when the first attempt is due and who receives an enquiry that comes in after hours. Choose who handles existing customers, active deals and requests that need product or pricing expertise. Test the intake with a new enquiry and a customer request so each goes to the right person.

The intake can be a CRM queue, a form alert or a shared inbox. If you use HubSpot, its documentation covers [assigning record owners](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/workflows/assign-and-rotate-record-owners-using-workflows) and [setting user notifications](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/user-management/how-to-set-up-user-notifications-in-hubspot). Check that the assigned caller and backup see the request and can open it.

If you have a batch of booth contacts to call after an event, see our [trade show lead follow-up service](/services/trade-show-lead-follow-up/). For old demo enquiries that have gone quiet, explore [lead reactivation](/services/lead-reactivation/).

## How to tell if your inbound follow-up is working

Pick five enquiries from the first week. Check when each arrived, when a caller first tried to reach the buyer and what happened after that. If a request missed its agreed response window, find out whether it waited for an owner, a call or a specialist answer.

Track the first attempt and the first conversation separately. A voicemail shows that a caller made an attempt. A conversation tells you whether the buyer is ready to move forward. Count requests with no call attempt on their own so you can see how many are still waiting.

Review booked meetings with sales. Which ones held? Did the seller have enough context to start the conversation? Pull one good handoff and one that needed more work. Use those examples to adjust what the caller asks and what gets passed on.
