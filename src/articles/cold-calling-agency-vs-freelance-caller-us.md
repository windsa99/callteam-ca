A freelance appointment setter is a good fit when your manager has a list, a tested opening and time to supervise the work. Hire CallTeam when your manager needs a team to run the campaign. We agree with sales on what qualifies a meeting. For each booking, your seller gets the buyer’s reason for meeting, what they agreed to discuss and any open questions. Your sales manager can use that feedback to see where calls are working and where the offer needs a clearer explanation.

## Check what your company already has ready

Before requesting quotes, check six inputs:

* A defined group of US businesses you can serve.
* A list or an agreed plan for obtaining suitable accounts and contacts.
* Approved product claims and a clear reason for the first call.
* Access to the calling tools, CRM and calendar required for the work.
* An internal contact who can resolve questions and review progress.
* Salespeople with time to attend meetings and continue the discussion.

Assign an owner to every item on the list. Include any task your team needs help with in the quote request.

For the separate decision about adding employees, use the [in-house SDR versus outsourced team guide](/articles/outsourced-sdr-vs-in-house-sdr-team/).

## Write a useful freelance cold-calling brief

Use this example to give a candidate enough detail to estimate the work.

| Brief item | Example of useful detail |
| --- | --- |
| Offer | Scheduling software for commercial field-service companies |
| Target audience | US businesses meeting the agreed size, service and territory criteria |
| Starting materials | Reviewed account sample, product overview and approved conversation outline |
| Assignment | Reach relevant contacts, ask the agreed discovery questions and arrange suitable sales discussions |
| Time allocation | A defined weekly budget covering preparation, calls, callbacks and CRM notes |
| Tools and access | Client-approved calling account, limited CRM access and seller availability |
| Support contact | Named sales lead for product questions and review decisions |
| Completion record | Work completed, buyer responses, open commitments and recommended next actions |

Ask which tasks cost extra and add them to the written scope before the trial.

## Evaluate the person who will make the calls

Look for experience with the buyer and sales situation you are targeting. A caller who has booked consumer appointments may need different preparation to discuss a technical B2B service with an operations leader.

Ask for a permitted example of their work and an explanation of how they handled an uncertain answer, a referral or a prospect who was outside the brief. Discuss how they record the next action and which questions they escalate to the client.

For a paid trial, agree on hours, pay and review criteria up front. Check how the caller prepares, handles a referral and records a promised callback.

Apply the same scrutiny to an agency. Ask who will be assigned, what training they receive and how the manager checks their work. For broader provider research, consult the [US cold-calling company comparison](/articles/best-b2b-cold-calling-companies-usa/).

Suppose you buy 40 hours in a month at US$45 per hour. Your own manager spends another 10 hours on list review, coaching and follow-up. Use your actual pay rates and the responsibilities in each quote.

## Plan for callbacks, absences and changes

A prospect may return a call after the booked work session ends. Decide who monitors those responses, when the caller becomes available again and how urgent requests reach your sales team.

Ask an individual about concurrent commitments and practical coverage. A freelancer may have a reliable arrangement for continuity, but it should be discussed before work begins. Ask an agency whether its proposed backup is included, how handovers work and whether another caller will have enough account context.

Keep the materials and records your business is entitled to use in accessible, approved systems. If someone becomes unavailable, the next person should be able to identify the prospect, read the conversation history and honor any outstanding commitment.

## Set the requirements for US prospecting

Describe your target territory and the hours when prospects and your sellers are available. Coverage of East Coast accounts can create a different schedule from a campaign focused on the West Coast. Confirm how referrals across regions will be handled.

If your policy requires US-based callers, put it in the brief. Ask the candidate to explain your offer back to you before they contact buyers.

Specify approved contact sources, calling methods, account exclusions and the process for honoring requests to stop contact. Assign responsibility for reviewing the requirements that apply to the planned campaign. The [calling compliance guide](/articles/b2b-cold-calling-compliance-global/) can help your team organize that review.
