Ask what you’re paying for before you compare quotes. A booking, an attended call and a qualified sales meeting have different value. Check which of those the agency bills for and who handles the work between them.

## When a monthly retainer makes sense

A retainer pays for an agreed scope over a set period. A managed team can adjust the target list, review calls and follow up with buyers who need time. This suits a complex offer or a new market where you need to learn who responds. Ask how much calling the agency can do each week and what it will review with your team.

Ask for weekly counts of meetings that took place and meetings sales accepted. Get the reasons for rejections. If the campaign keeps reaching the wrong accounts, ask what the agency will change the following week.

## When to pay per meeting

Pay per meeting can fit a narrow campaign with a clear buyer and simple rules for who qualifies. Put in writing when you owe the fee. Ask how the agency handles repeated bookings with the same account, cancellations and contacts already in your pipeline.

If you pay for bookings, budget for no-shows. If you pay for held meetings, agree what happens when only the wrong buyer attends. If you pay for sales-accepted meetings, agree what Sales needs to see and how long it has to reject a meeting.

Fixed projects can work for an event follow-up or a list with a known end date. Hourly pricing buys caller time and may leave list preparation, coaching and coverage with your team. A hybrid quote combines a monthly base fee with a fee for a specified result. Add both before comparing.

## What counts as booked, held and sales-accepted?

- **Booked** means the prospect agreed to a meeting and the team sent an invite.
- **Held** means the intended prospect attended.
- **Sales-accepted** means sales confirmed that the held meeting met the agreed criteria, such as account fit, buyer role and a business reason to continue.

Decide how long sales has to review the handoff. Give the seller a place to record why a meeting was rejected and share those reasons with the callers. Apply the same rules to both quotes. Use the [qualified appointment standard](/articles/what-makes-b2b-appointment-qualified-practical-standard/) to write the criteria, or run the [meeting quality check](/b2b-meeting-quality-check/) on your own numbers.

## What should go into the full campaign cost?

For named providers and their published billing terms, use the [B2B lead generation agency pricing report](/resources/b2b-lead-generation-pricing-report/).

If you want to buy meetings, [compare pay-per-appointment companies](/resources/best-pay-per-appointment-companies/) on setup fees, minimum purchases and no-show rules.

Include setup, the agency fee, data, tools, and the time your staff spend managing the campaign and preparing for meetings. If you compare a new quarter with an existing campaign, count the same costs in both. A one-off setup fee belongs in your first-term comparison even when it falls outside the monthly fee.

Research effort goes up when the market is small, buyers are senior or accounts need individual preparation. Extra regions, languages and follow-up can also change the scope. Ask who pays for list updates and who provides cover when a caller is away.

Track the meetings booked during the period you paid for. Give late bookings time to take place before you calculate the costs. If you booked ten meetings in March and two take place in April, keep those two with March’s bookings. Leave cost per meeting blank if the count at that stage is zero.

## Appointment-setting quote worksheet

Use the fields below or <a href="/assets/downloads/appointment-setting-quote-worksheet.csv" download="CallTeam-Appointment-Setting-Quote-Worksheet.csv">download the quote worksheet (CSV)</a>. Fill in the price and scope first. Update the results after sales reviews the meetings.

| Field | What to write down |
| --- | --- |
| Fees | Setup, monthly or per-meeting fee, and required extras |
| Work you own | Data, CRM, seller time and campaign management |
| Billing trigger | Booked, held or sales-accepted, with the exact definition |
| No-shows and rejects | Who follows up, what qualifies for replacement and when you can reject |
| Meeting results | Booked, held and sales-accepted counts for the same group |
| Cost at each stage | Total spend divided by each of those three counts |

Ask each supplier for a sample handoff and a weekly report with client details removed. Check whether the report shows who attended and what sales rejected. Use the [agency proposal comparison tool](/lead-generation-proposal-comparison/) when you have quotes in hand.

## Use the costs to make a buying decision

Reject a low-price quote if the supplier cannot explain who fixes a failed handoff. When scopes differ, add the cost of completing the work your team would take on. Then decide whether the extra work fits the team’s schedule and budget.

Set a review date before you sign. Compare attendance, acceptance, rejection reasons and early opportunity progress. If sellers attend good meetings that rarely turn into opportunities, look at the offer, timing and qualification questions before buying more appointments.
