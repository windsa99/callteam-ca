“I’m not the right person” is useful information. The caller’s ownership assumption may be wrong, the account may use a buying committee, or the contact may be involved without holding final approval.

Do not argue from a job title. Accept the correction, ask for the responsible role, and preserve the route accurately.

## The immediate response

Use a short role-first question.

> Thanks for correcting me. Which team or role normally owns this?

If the contact answers and the conversation remains open, ask:

> Would it be better for me to contact them directly, and may I say you pointed me to that function?

The second question separates a routing correction from permission to mention the conversation. It does not ask the person to sell internally on your behalf.

Start with the [Cold Call Objection Handling Database](/articles/cold-call-objection-handling/) when the buyer's resistance has not been classified. This page owns wrong-person routing. The [gatekeeper guide](/articles/how-to-get-past-b2b-gatekeeper-without-sounding-deceptive/) owns access control before the intended buyer is reached.

## Do not challenge the buyer with their title

Online data may show that the person leads the department, controls a budget, or reports to an executive. That does not prove they own this decision.

Avoid:

> Your title says you are responsible for this, so I think you are the right person.

Use:

> It sounds like someone else owns this area. Which function would be more accurate?

The first version tells the buyer their organization is wrong. The second invites a correction.

## Determine what “not the right person” means

The contact may occupy one of several positions in the decision.

| Meaning | Useful question | Next action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Completely uninvolved | “Which team normally owns it?” | Correct the function and end cleanly |
| User or affected team | “How does the issue show up for your team?” | Learn context, then map the owner |
| Technical evaluator | “Who owns the business decision beside your review?” | Add the economic or operating owner |
| Influencer or sponsor | “Who leads the evaluation after you identify the need?” | Preserve both roles |
| Not the final approver | “Who else has to approve, and what do they evaluate?” | Map the committee |
| Polite exit | “Understood. Should I close the topic entirely?” | Respect the no and stop |

Do not force the buyer into a category. Use one question and listen.

## Ask for the role before the name

“Who is the decision-maker?” can sound like the caller wants to bypass the person. It also assumes one person owns the whole decision.

Role-first questions are easier to answer:

* Which team owns this workflow?
* Who is responsible for the business outcome?
* Who evaluates the technical requirements?
* Does Finance, security, legal, or procurement join the decision?
* Is there a program or transformation leader coordinating the work?

Once the role is clear, ask whether there is a specific contact. A function without a name still improves account research.

## Preserve the original contact’s real involvement

The person may not approve the purchase but may understand the current process, use the solution, validate risk, or influence whether the project survives.

Ask:

> Are you outside the decision entirely, or would your team still be involved in evaluating or using it?

This prevents two mistakes. The caller does not discard an important stakeholder, and sales does not mistake an influencer for the economic buyer.

Use the [buying committee guide](/articles/map-b2b-buying-committee/) when the decision spans users, technical evaluators, executives, Finance, security, legal, or procurement.

## Represent the referral accurately

There are four different outcomes:

1. “That belongs to Operations.”
2. “Speak with the VP Operations.”
3. “You can say I pointed you to the VP Operations.”
4. “I will introduce you to the VP Operations.”

Do not turn the first outcome into the fourth. A function, name, permission, and introduction carry different levels of context.

When contacting the next person, say exactly what happened:

> I spoke briefly with [Name], who said this sits with Operations. I am reaching out about [specific question].

Do not say the colleague recommended your company unless they did.

## Use buyer-specific routing

The right route depends on the decision:

* Financial impact, controls, and funding may lead to the CFO or Finance owner.
* Architecture, integration, and IT operations may lead to the CIO or technical team.
* Security governance and risk may lead to the CISO.
* Workflow, capacity, and execution may lead to Operations.
* Commercial process and supplier approval may lead to procurement.
* Strategic, cross-functional outcomes may require the CEO or another executive sponsor.

The [Buyer Playbooks hub](/resources/outbound-playbooks/buyers/) provides the language and discovery path for each role. Use it after the account map is corrected.

## Qualify before transferring or booking

A referral is not a qualified B2B lead. Before asking for a meeting, confirm as much as the conversation reasonably allows:

* the account fits the ICP;
* the issue or decision is relevant;
* the corrected role actually owns part of the process;
* the original contact’s involvement is understood;
* the next person has a credible reason to hear the question;
* permission and referral language are recorded;
* the meeting has a clear purpose.

If the contact knows only the role, research the person before making the next call. Do not book the original contact into a meeting simply because they answered.

## Record the account correction

Update the CRM with:

* original contact and role;
* corrected function and named person, when provided;
* original contact’s involvement;
* whether the account-level issue was confirmed;
* permission to mention the conversation;
* introduction offered or not offered;
* contact preferences or suppression requirements;
* next research or outreach action.

This data improves targeting beyond one call. Repeated wrong-person outcomes may show that the campaign is using the wrong title, company size, department, or offer language.

## Know when to stop

Stop when the contact says they are uninvolved and will not route the call, repeats the refusal, becomes irritated, or asks not to be contacted. Stop when the account is outside the ICP or the issue is not relevant.

Use a clean exit:

> Understood. Thanks for correcting the route. I will not keep you on the call.

The conversation can still be useful even without a name. An accurate function and a clean exit protect the account relationship.

B2B lead generation depends on better account intelligence after every conversation. Data providers and AI can suggest likely buyers. Human calls reveal how responsibility is actually divided, which people influence the decision, and whether the account has a real reason to continue.
