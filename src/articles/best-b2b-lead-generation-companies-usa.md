## Which provider fits your US campaign?

### Belkins for multichannel appointment setting

Calling coverage varies by [package](https://belkins.io/pricing). Partners deliver the small-business option. Ask which team gets your account and who handles replies and reschedules.

### EBQ for inbound follow-up and outbound calling

EBQ covers both types of outreach. Its [published fees](https://ebq.com/ebq-pricing/) are $5,000 a month for half-time capacity and $10,000 for full-time capacity, with an annual commitment. Ask how the team splits its hours between your inbound queue and target accounts.

### MarketStar for enterprise sales operations

MarketStar covers pipeline creation, deal execution and customer expansion. Ask which services your team would use if your main need is new sales meetings.

### memoryBlue for technology sales development

memoryBlue serves B2B and government technology sellers. It also offers recruiting and training and [acquired Operatix in 2023](https://memoryblue.com/blog/memoryblue-acquires-operatix-to-create-a-global-leader-in-sales-acceleration-services-for-the-b2b-technology-ecosystem/). Ask which team would handle US accounts and who manages those callers.

### Televerde for lead nurturing and SDR support

Televerde follows up with marketing leads as well as outbound accounts. If you need both, ask how the team routes replies and decides when a lead is ready for sales. See the [Televerde vs memoryBlue comparison](/compare/televerde-vs-memoryblue/) for delivery details and questions to ask before signing.

## Define the US campaign before requesting quotes

Specify the states, time zones and buyer roles you want to reach. If callers must be based in the US, put that requirement in the brief and ask the provider to name the assigned team.

Give the provider your approved offer, existing accounts, exclusions and current opportunities. Ask how it will avoid duplicate outreach, route replies and manage follow-up around your sales calendar. Clear exclusions protect relationships as well as reporting quality.

## Require a useful sales handoff

Check how quickly meeting notes reach the seller. If the booking lands on the same day, ask who alerts the seller and how they see any last-minute changes.

An agreed handoff should identify the person, account fit, business reason, meeting purpose, timing and unresolved issues. Let sales distinguish a confirmed need from the caller's hypothesis, and give it a consistent way to accept or query a booking.

For illustration, a US software campaign might find an operations leader willing to examine a reporting bottleneck while security approval remains outstanding. Record both points so the seller can prepare the right conversation. Counting that contact as a completed opportunity would lose the distinction between interest and qualification.

Use the [qualified appointment standard](/articles/what-makes-b2b-appointment-qualified-practical-standard/) and [Meeting Quality Check](/b2b-meeting-quality-check/) to agree the evidence before launch. Request reporting for bookings, attendance and sales progress separately so a busy calendar can be assessed properly.

## Who handles contact rules?

Ask how the provider manages approved claims, requests to stop contact, data sources, caller identification and any recording or dialing technology. The FTC's [B2B telemarketing protections](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/03/ftc-implements-new-protections-businesses-against-telemarketing-fraud-affirms-protections-against-ai) are one reason to avoid a blanket assumption that business calls sit outside regulation.

Name the person who will keep your contact exclusions current and tell callers when a claim or script changes.
