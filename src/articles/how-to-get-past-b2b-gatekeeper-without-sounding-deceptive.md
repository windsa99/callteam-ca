Gatekeepers are not castle guards. They are people with a phone, a queue of interruptions and a very good reason not to trust another caller saying, "It is a personal business matter."

Getting past a B2B gatekeeper is not about defeating anyone. It is about sounding credible enough that the person answering can route you, correct you or tell you the truth.

## Why most gatekeeper scripts fail

Bad gatekeeper advice teaches callers to act mysterious, borrow authority or pretend the prospect expects them. The transfer becomes the only goal, so common sense gets thrown out the window.

Even when the trick works, the buyer arrives annoyed. The assistant learns the caller lied. The CRM celebrates a connection while the account quietly writes the company off.

The caller's real job is smaller: explain the business reason, identify the likely owner and ask for help reaching the right place.

## Use a four-part gatekeeper response

A strong response has four parts: your name, your company, the business issue and the routing question. It should fit inside one calm breath.

> Hi, this is [Name] with [Company]. I am calling about how the team handles [specific business issue]. Would [Buyer Name] own that, or is there somebody else I should speak with?

That sentence is commercial without becoming a commercial. It gives the gatekeeper enough information to help and leaves room to correct the contact.

## What to say when asked, "What is this regarding?"

Do not answer with a product category nobody asked for. "Enterprise transformation solutions" is not a reason. It is fog wearing a necktie.

Try one of these:

> It is about after-hours IT coverage for the locations. I am trying to find who reviews that service.

> It is about the manual handoff between recruiting and interview scheduling. Would talent operations own that?

> We help manufacturers prepare software opportunities for security review. I am trying to reach the person who owns vendor evaluation.

If the gatekeeper asks whether it is a sales call, answer honestly:

> It is a business development call. I am not asking you to pretend otherwise. I am trying to find out whether this issue belongs with [role].

That will not produce every transfer. It will keep the company's credibility attached to the call.

## Three bad responses and the repair

**Bad: "They are expecting my call."** Use it only when they truly are. Otherwise say, "We have not spoken. I am calling because [account reason]."

**Bad: "It is confidential."** If the matter can be pitched cold, it can usually be described in one business sentence. Give the category and ask for the owner.

**Bad: "Just put me through."** The gatekeeper has no reason to accept the command. Replace it with, "Could you help me confirm whether this belongs with [role]?"

The repair is not softer language for the same trick. It is a truthful reason that lets the other person make a sensible decision.

## Treat executive assistants like business operators

An executive assistant may understand the executive's priorities, calendar and decision process better than the caller understands the account. Talking down to that person is commercially stupid.

Give the assistant a sharper version of the buyer case:

> I am reaching out because [verified account event] may create [specific operating issue]. We support [buyer type] with [defined outcome]. Is that relevant to [Executive], or would another leader assess it first?

Listen to the correction. "Send it to operations" is not a defeat. It is free account intelligence delivered by somebody who knows the route.

## Handle the five common gatekeeper outcomes

**Transfer:** Thank the person, then start fresh with the buyer. Do not claim the gatekeeper endorsed the offer.

**Different contact:** Confirm the name, title and best path. Ask one question at a time instead of conducting an interrogation at reception.

**Email first:** Ask what would help the recipient judge relevance, send the short note and record the follow-up date.

**Call later:** Confirm whether the issue is timing, absence or policy. A real date belongs in the CRM; "sometime next quarter" belongs in fiction.

**No access:** Respect the instruction. Try an approved public channel only if the account remains valid and the request was not a do-not-contact direction.

## Know when to stop

Continue when the person is helping define the owner, process or timing. Redirect when the role is wrong. Stop when there is a clear refusal, a contact restriction, an explicit opt-out or evidence the offer does not fit.

Do not turn persistence into repeated annoyance. The [cold call objection handling guide](/articles/cold-call-objection-handling/) explains how to separate a reflex from a firm decision without arguing with either one.

## Record the route in the CRM

Useful gatekeeper notes include the correct department, buyer title, direct line policy, preferred channel, timing, named contact, routing language and any restriction. Mark whether each point was confirmed or inferred.

Never write "decision-maker blocked us" when the caller asked for the wrong person using a vague reason. The note should help the next caller improve, not protect the first caller's ego.

## CallTeam field card: gatekeeper script

Copy this version and replace the brackets before calling:

> Hi, [Name] with [Company]. I am calling about [one business issue] for [team or location]. I was trying to reach [buyer or role]. Would that sit with them, or can you point me in the right direction?

If asked whether it is sales:

> Yes, it is a business development call. The specific reason is [issue]. If that is not relevant there, I am happy to take the correction.

If refused:

> Understood. Is there an approved way to send a short note, or should I close this out?
