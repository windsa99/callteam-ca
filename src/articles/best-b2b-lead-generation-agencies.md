## What to ask about a SaaS quote

Give each agency the same 90-day brief, account list and buyer roles. Ask how it estimated bookings and attendance. Have both teams itemize setup, data and recurring fees.

A **held, qualified demo** takes place with the right buyer at a company that fits. The buyer confirms a problem your product can solve, and your sales team accepts the meeting against the agreed criteria.

### Three questions to ask both agencies

<ol class="saas-agency-questions">
  <li><strong>Who will make the calls?</strong> Ask who is assigned and who handles callbacks, confirmations and rescheduling.</li>
  <li><strong>What counts as a qualified demo?</strong> Agree on account fit, buyer role and meeting purpose. Decide how to count no-shows and rejected meetings.</li>
  <li><strong>What will my seller get before the demo?</strong> Ask for a sample with customer details removed. Look for the buyer’s role, confirmed problem, meeting purpose and open questions.</li>
</ol>

## Where each provider fits

### Belkins for multichannel appointment setting

Calling coverage varies by [Belkins package](https://belkins.io/pricing), and partner agencies deliver its small-business option. Ask who will run your campaign and which channels the quote includes.

### Callbox for multiple territories

Callbox offers multichannel campaigns and international coverage. Ask which languages, time zones and account groups the assigned team can cover.

### CIENCE for separate team, platform and SDR costs

CIENCE prices the campaign team, platform and SDRs separately. Its pricing page describes setup and performance bonuses. Ask for the setup fee and how the bonus is calculated.

### memoryBlue for technology sales development

memoryBlue offers SDR execution, recruitment and training for technology companies. It [acquired Operatix in 2023](https://memoryblue.com/blog/memoryblue-acquires-operatix-to-create-a-global-leader-in-sales-acceleration-services-for-the-b2b-technology-ecosystem/). Ask which services and team are in your proposal. See the [memoryBlue alternatives comparison](/compare/memoryblue-alternatives/) for other team models.

### SalesHive for phone-only or phone-and-email outreach

SalesHive offers both channel options and distinguishes US-based and offshore SDRs. The rate depends on the plan and staffing. See the [SalesHive vs CallTeam comparison](/compare/saleshive-alternatives/) for a closer look.

## Define the handoff your sellers need

Before appointing an agency, write down the information that should accompany a meeting. Include the account fit, buyer's role, reason for discussing the offer, timing, agreed purpose, outstanding questions and next owner. Our [appointment-setting service](/services/b2b-appointment-setting/) explains how those responsibilities connect to booking and follow-up.

An illustrative handoff might say that an operations director wants to examine a costly reconciliation workflow, while finance approval and the implementation date remain unconfirmed. Those unknowns give sales something useful to investigate. A calendar invitation saying only that the prospect is interested leaves the seller to reconstruct the conversation.

Agree how sales will report acceptance, attendance and the next commercial step. The [outbound appointment-setting guide](/articles/outbound-appointment-setting/) develops that quality standard, while our [AI lead generation explanation](/articles/ai-lead-generation/) describes the supporting research layer.

## Compare the full agency cost

Use the same currency, evaluation period, data requirements and staffing assumptions for each proposal. Separate one-time setup charges, recurring service fees, software, variable payments and work your own team must perform.

For example, 13 four-week billing periods cover 52 weeks, while a calendar-month agreement normally has 12 monthly charges in a year. That arithmetic matters for a long-running engagement, although the actual contract determines what you pay and for how long.

Ask each agency how it bills for a replacement meeting. For example, a buyer may attend but fall outside your agreed company size. If the agency replaces that meeting, confirm whether the replacement carries an extra fee and whether it must happen before the contract ends.
