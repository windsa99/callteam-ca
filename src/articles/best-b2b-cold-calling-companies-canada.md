Choosing a B2B cold calling company in Canada means deciding how much of that work you want the provider to manage. A project calling service, a telephone appointment-setting firm and an outsourced sales development team can leave very different responsibilities with your business.

## Decide what you want the calling company to take over

Write down your starting position before requesting quotes. An Ontario industrial supplier with a researched account list needs a different scope from a software company still working out which buyers to approach.

Use one of these starting briefs:

* **We have usable accounts and approved messaging.** Specify the calling, callbacks, qualification and reporting you need.
* **We know the market but need the accounts researched.** Include list preparation, review and approval before calling starts.
* **We need the whole sales development function managed.** Include campaign planning, caller management, data, follow-up, booking and sales feedback.

Describe any work your own team will retain. If your account executive will confirm meetings, say so. If the provider must handle rescheduling, include it. The [prospect list buying guide](/articles/b2b-prospect-list-building-services/) covers the data specification when research forms part of the quote.

## Give each finalist the same short calling exercise

A prepared sales presentation tells you how a provider sells its own service. A short, fictional calling scenario helps you understand how the team would represent yours.

Share a one-page offer brief and ask for a walkthrough. Use invented account details and have a colleague play the buyer. Keep the exercise within your team.

| Moment in the exercise | What to ask the provider to demonstrate |
| --- | --- |
| The buyer asks why they are calling | A clear reason tied to the account and offer |
| The buyer uses an unfamiliar technical term | A sensible clarifying question and an appropriate escalation |
| The buyer already has a supplier | A relevant question about circumstances that could justify a discussion |
| The buyer falls outside your service area | Accurate disqualification and a clear record |
| The buyer agrees to meet | A useful meeting purpose and the right details for sales |
| The call ends | A concise note and an assigned next action |

Ask who coaches this work and how the provider would address a weak response. If the demonstration comes from a senior manager, ask how the assigned callers will be prepared to meet the same standard.

## Put the same scope into every Canadian calling proposal

Comparisons become more useful when every provider quotes against the same brief. Complete this worksheet before the final discussion.

| Item | Detail to supply or request |
| --- | --- |
| Target market | Account type, territory, buyer roles and exclusions |
| Calling coverage | Prospect time zones, working days, callback handling and languages |
| Team | Caller assignment, backup arrangements, manager and review process |
| Data | Client list or provider research, approval method and ownership |
| Work included | Calls, messages, callbacks, scheduling, confirmation and reporting |
| Acceptance | What qualifies a meeting and who reviews disputed records |
| Commercial terms | Setup, recurring or project charges, minimum commitment, currency and cancellation |
| Client obligations | Product training, approvals, calendar capacity and sales feedback |

For a Canadian quote, confirm whether the amount is in CAD or USD, which taxes apply and whether technology or data charges sit outside the service fee. Request a complete campaign total for the proposed scope. Public pricing from a different package may leave out work your team requires.

When a provider will use your CRM, link the proposal to the [CRM integration checklist](/articles/appointment-setting-crm-integration/) so record handling is settled before outreach begins.

## What should you ask about Canadian calling rules?

Ask the provider to explain the process for the numbers, markets and calling technology in your campaign. The CRTC [states that calls to business numbers](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/phone/telemarketing/exempt.htm) are exempt from the National DNCL Rules while remaining subject to Telemarketing and ADAD Rules.

Its [telemarketing requirements](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/phone/telemarketing/tobligations/rules-regles.htm) also cover registration and other operating obligations. Obtain a clear explanation of caller identification, calling hours and handling requests to stop contact.

If email or text is included, assess the requirements for those channels separately. The [Canadian agency comparison](/articles/best-b2b-lead-generation-companies-canada/) provides broader channel and market context.
